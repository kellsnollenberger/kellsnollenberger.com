House rules & how advice works

Five fair six-sided dice. A 3 scores zero; all other faces score their value. Lock at least one die per roll, then reroll the rest. If your locked score exceeds the lowest posted score—or every legal hold on a new roll would exceed it—your turn immediately ends as a bust. Matching the score remains alive for a playoff. Lowest total wins. Every seat antes before rolling.

The winner starts the next round; play continues in fixed circular seat order. The initial leader is you. A tie carries the entire pot into a playoff. Only players tied for the lowest score add another ante and play again. Their previous turn order reverses: the last person to tie goes first. This applies to two-way, three-way, or larger ties; another tie repeats the rule among the remaining tied players. The eventual winner takes the whole pot and leads the next regular round. Seats unable to afford the regular-round ante sit out. If a tied player cannot afford a playoff ante, the playoff pauses with its pot intact; Table settings can reset the session. No money is deducted until all tied players can ante.

Without a posted score, the engine minimizes expected final score. With posted scores, it maximizes the chance of winning outright or reaching a playoff, accounting for posted scores and players still to act. Among equally likely ways to stay alive, it favors winning outright. All possible future rolls in your turn are evaluated exactly.

Unplayed opponents are forecast using a minimum-expected-score strategy. Actual computers adapt to posted scores, so displayed win and tie probabilities are estimates when players remain. Last-to-act win and tie probabilities for the current pass are exact. Eventual playoff-win probabilities and extra ante costs are not modeled by the advice objective. Advice is conditional on following the suggested strategy on later rolls. “Best” means best for the stated objective, not a guaranteed win.

No extra betting beyond playoff antes, zero bonuses, or shoot-the-moon rule. Changing settings begins a new session; refreshing clears this table.
