House rules & how advice works

Five fair six-sided dice. A 3 scores zero; all other faces score their value. Lock at least one die per roll, then reroll the rest. If your locked score exceeds the lowest posted score—or every legal hold on a new roll would exceed it—your turn immediately ends as a bust, unless five sixes is still possible. Holding only sixes keeps the moon chase alive; once you keep another face, normal bust rules apply. Matching the score remains alive for a push. Lowest total wins. Every seat antes before rolling.

The winner starts the next round; play continues in fixed circular seat order. The initial leader is you. A tie carries the entire pot into a push. Only players tied for the lowest score add another ante and play again. Their previous turn order reverses: the last person to tie goes first. This applies to two-way, three-way, or larger ties; another tie repeats the rule among the remaining tied players. One tie is a push; two consecutive ties are a double push, then a triple push, and so on. With a $5 ante, each remaining player has contributed $5 initially, $10 after the first push, $15 after a double push, then another $5 for every further push. Eliminated players’ earlier antes stay in the pot. The eventual winner takes the whole pot and leads the next regular round. Seats unable to afford the regular-round ante sit out. If a tied player cannot afford a push ante, the push pauses with its pot intact; Table settings can reset the session. No money is deducted until all tied players can ante.

Your advice maximizes modeled expected round profit: expected payout less all your antes, including extra push antes. It accounts for posted scores, reversed push order, multiway and repeated ties. Each remaining roll in your current turn is enumerated exactly. All possible future rolls in your turn are evaluated exactly.

Unplayed opponents use the same target-aware policy as the actual computers: minimize expected score when leading; otherwise maximize winning or tying, using outright wins to break equal-survival choices. Future push turns assume this policy for everyone, including you. Repeated ties are solved without a fixed cutoff, with reversed order after every tie. This is a continuation model, not a proof of globally optimal play: you may choose differently or follow the improved EV advice in later pushes. The model assumes players can cover every required push ante; the actual game pauses if anyone cannot. Advice is conditional on following the suggested strategy on later rolls. “Best EV” means the best current-turn expected return under this continuation model, not a guaranteed win.

Shoot the moon: five sixes scores zero, including sixes saved across multiple rolls. A moon ties any other zero; the usual push rules apply. No extra betting or additional zero bonuses. Changing settings begins a new session; refreshing clears this table.
